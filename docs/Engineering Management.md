In our **Housing Quality & Safety Certification Program**, the most important patterns are stakeholder-related because the project depends on trust, transparency, and cooperation between students, landlords, the university, and legal/insurance entities.

## **Negative patterns** 

### **1\. Lack of transparency**

This is one of the biggest risks in our **Housing Quality & Safety Certification Program**. Students rely on the system to make safe housing decisions, so unclear or hidden information would quickly destroy trust.

**Why it’s problematic:**

* Students may choose unsafe housing due to missing or unclear data  
* The system loses credibility  
* Stakeholders (university, legal entities) cannot rely on the data

**How we would handle it:**

* Show **clear safety indicators** (e.g., certified / not certified / risk level)

* Display **complaints and inspection results transparently**

* Provide **simple summaries \+ detailed reports (optional)**

* Ensure all reports are **accurate and verifiable (e.g., signed PDFs)**

### **2\. Ignoring stakeholder feedback**

If we don’t involve users during development, we risk building something that doesn’t actually solve their problems.

**Why it’s problematic:**

* The system may be too complex or impractical  
* Students may not trust or use it  
* Landlords may resist adopting it

**How we would handle it:**

* Organize **regular feedback sessions** with:

  * Students (end users)  
  * Head of Student Housing (internal client)

* Run **testing phases (prototypes, pilot program)**

* Continuously **adjust features based on feedback**

### **3\. Poor conflict handling**

Conflicts are expected—especially with landlords whose properties may receive complaints or fail inspections.

**Why it’s problematic:**

* Landlords may feel unfairly treated  
* They might avoid participating in the system  
* Could create legal or reputational issues

**How we would handle it:**

* Add **resolution mechanisms**, such as:

  * “ Issue Resolved” / “Landlord Resolution” status  
* Show **history of improvements**, not just problems

* Ensure **fair and objective certification criteria**
