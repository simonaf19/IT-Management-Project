# **1\. Project Charter** {#1.-project-charter}

The project proposes a Housing Quality & Safety Certification Program for student rental housing. Its goal is to reduce uncertainty in the rental market by introducing independent inspections, clear certification standards, and a public registry where tenants can verify housing safety before signing a contract.

The main client is the University of Primorska, with the Head of Student Housing as the internal client. Possible partners include the Municipality of Koper, the Student Organization of UP, and real estate agencies. The project supports student welfare, improves transparency, and encourages landlords to maintain safer properties.

The planned deliverables include a Housing Quality Standard, an inspection and certification procedure, a public web registry, and a complaint/reporting mechanism. The pilot is planned for 12 months, with expansion to additional cities over 24 months. The estimated budget is 15,000 euros.

# **2\. Personas and Problem Scenarios** {#2.-personas-and-problem-scenarios}

Three main personas were created to represent the users and stakeholders of the system: Antonio González, an international student tenant; Matjaž Horvat, an independent landlord; and Nina Kovač, a municipal housing officer.

Antonio represents students who fear hidden housing problems such as mold, humidity, or unsafe conditions. He needs reliable inspection information, certification history, tenant reviews, and risk summaries before renting.

Matjaž represents landlords seeking to demonstrate that their apartments are safe and well-maintained. Certification would help him build trust with tenants, reduce disputes, and protect his reputation with objective inspection records.

Nina represents public authorities that need centralized housing data. The system would help her monitor recurring issues, generate reports, and move from reactive complaint handling toward preventive housing management.

# **3\. User Stories and Prototyping** {#3.-user-stories-and-prototyping}

The user stories define what each persona should be able to do in the system. Tenants should be able to verify certification status, review inspection results, read complaints and reviews, and report problems. Landlords should be able to apply for certification, schedule inspections, download reports, and create move-in condition records. Municipal officers should be able to monitor properties, track risks, generate reports, and receive alerts for serious issues.

The prototype focuses on making safety information easy to understand. Important features include property search, inspection history, certification validity, complaint tracking, reviews, safety summaries, and role-based access for administrative users.

The main design idea is to replace long technical explanations with clear visual indicators, simple summaries, timelines, and dashboards.

# **4\. Hypothesis Testing** {#4.-hypothesis-testing}

Interviews were conducted with a parent of a student tenant, an international student, and a young professional. The parent strongly valued safety certification, inspection records, and verified reviews because these would increase trust when choosing housing for a child.

The international student focused mainly on price and availability, but still found tenant reviews and inspection information useful. This showed that the platform should remain free and simple for students, because many are not willing to pay more for certified apartments.

The young professional emphasized transparent total costs, accurate photos, location, parking, and landlord reliability. This confirmed the need for verified listings, clear cost information, and a way to report issues publicly.

Overall, the interviews supported the project idea: users want trustworthy information, but it must be presented simply and should not make housing more expensive for tenants.

# **5\. Usability Testing** {#5.-usability-testing}

Usability testing evaluated whether users could find a property, check safety information, view certification status, read reviews and complaints, and identify risks. Most tasks were completed successfully within about one minute.

The main difficulty was understanding certification and inspection data. Parents wanted simple safety summaries, students relied more on reviews than technical inspection data, and young professionals were unsure whether to trust complaints or inspection results more.

The key improvements identified were: simplify inspection information, add clear safety indicators such as badges or colors, separate reviews from complaints, and provide a short risk summary. The testing showed that the system is usable, but safety information needs to be clearer for non-technical users.

# **6\. Scrum Basics and Implementation Plan** {#6.-scrum-basics-and-implementation-plan}

The Scrum plan focuses on improving the platform based on usability testing. Sprint 0 prepares the workspace, design system, and persona requirements. Sprint 1 focuses on safety visualization, certification simplification, and inspection readability. Sprint 2 separates reviews from complaints and adds risk summaries. Sprint 3 focuses on interface polishing and final documentation.

The team has already created a high-fidelity Figma prototype. The prototype includes a dashboard with simplified safety cards, a property inspection view with pass/fail badges, a structured complaints system, and clear navigation between Dashboard, Properties, Inspections, Complaints, Reports, and Register Property.

The implementation directly responds to user feedback by replacing complex safety data with clear indicators and short summaries.

# **7\. Project Stakeholders** {#7.-project-stakeholders}

The main stakeholders are the University of Primorska, the Head of Student Housing, landlords, legal and insurance entities, and students. The university and student housing office benefit from improved student welfare and fewer housing complaints. Landlords benefit from a trusted way to prove housing quality, while students benefit from safer and more transparent rental choices.

Possible conflicts include budget limitations, operational pressure, landlord concerns about failed inspections, and liability risks. The team's response is to keep communication regular, provide transparent data, design a simple interface, and make certification reports reliable and exportable.

# **8\. Engineering Management** {#8.-engineering-management}

The engineering management reflection identifies three negative patterns: unclear role distribution, uneven workload, and lack of proactive communication. These are duplicated efforts and late corrections.

The team proposes clearer ownership, shared task tracking, more open coordination, and reviewing each other’s work to reduce dependency on one person.

Three positive patterns were also highlighted: resolving conflicts constructively, using continuous feedback and improvement, and being a catalyst for team progress. These patterns help the team adapt, maintain momentum, and focus on the overall success of the project.

# **9\. Final Overall Summary** {#9.-final-overall-summary}

The joined homework document describes the development of a student rental housing certification platform from project idea to stakeholder analysis, personas, user stories, interviews, usability testing, Scrum planning, prototype implementation, and team reflection.

The core conclusion is that tenants, parents, landlords, and public officials all need more trustworthy and transparent information about rental housing. However, the system must present this information in a simple, visual, and user-friendly way. The most important final improvements are clear safety badges, short risk summaries, verified reviews, separated complaints, and accessible inspection records.
