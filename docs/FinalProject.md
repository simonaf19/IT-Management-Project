1. # **Project Charter** {#project-charter}

**Project code and name**   
**Code:** HQSCP-2026 **Name:** Housing Quality & Safety Certification Program

**Description**  
Finding a rental apartment is stressful enough, but for students and newcomers, it often comes with an extra layer of risk: misleading listings, hidden mold or structural issues, and landlords who vanish after you sign. There is currently no easy way for a renter to know whether an apartment is actually safe and livable before moving in.

This project builds a certification program that solves exactly that. We want to develop a system where apartments can be inspected and certified by trained, independent inspectors, and listed in a public online registry that anyone can check before renting. 

The program starts in one city (pilot), focused on student housing, and expands to other cities once the model is proven.

**Project client**  
**Contracting Authority:** University of Primorska  
**Internal Client:** Head of Student Housing  
**Possible partners:** Municipality of Koper, Student Organization of UP, and real estate agencies.  
The importance of the project for the organization and its environment   
The project strengthens the University’s credibility and supports student welfare by improving transparency in the rental market. Certified properties gain a competitive advantage, encouraging landlords to improve standards. The program increases safety for tenants and contributes to higher overall housing quality in the region.

**The purpose and the objectives of the project**  
**Purpose:** Build a working certification system for rental apartments: from inspection standards to a public registry of verified properties.

**Objectives:**

* Define clear safety and habitability criteria for rental housing  
* Train independent housing inspectors  
* Launch a public online registry of certified properties  
* Certify at least 200 apartments in the pilot city within 12 months  
* Expand to 3 additional cities by month 24

**Possible requirements of the contracting authority**  
  a. DESCRIPTION OF THE FINISHED PRODUCT, SERVICE, OR RESULT 

The project will deliver:

* A defined Housing Quality Standard (HQS)  
* A structured inspection and certification procedure  
* A public web-based registry of certified properties  
* A complaint and reporting mechanism

Certification will be valid for 12 months, and participation is voluntary.

 b. REPORTING AND FINAL REPORT   
The Project Manager will provide monthly progress reports to the Steering Committee, including:

* Timeline status  
* Budget overview  
* Risk updates  
* Key milestones achieved

A final project report will summarize implementation results, pilot outcomes, certified units, user feedback, financial overview, and recommendations for expansion.

**Project limitations**   
**a) Rough Schedule**   
12 months to full pilot launch. 24 months to multi-city expansion.  
**b) Available Funds**

| :---- | :---- |
| Technical development  | €3,500 |
| Inspection coordination & training Legal consultation | €2,000 €1,000 |

**c) Phases** 

1. **Planning (2 months):** Development of Housing Quality Standards and certification framework.  
2. **Preparation (3 months):** Organizational setup, inspector training preparation, and registry setup.  
3. **Pilot implementation (5 months):** Training inspectors, launching the registry, and conducting initial inspections.  
4. **Evaluation (2 months):** Review of results, user feedback, and adjustments.  
5. **Public launch and expansion planning (2 months):** Official launch and preparation for expansion.

**d) Milestones**

1. Housing Quality Standard approved – April 2026

2. Online registry launched – January 2027

3. First certified apartment published – January 2027

4. Pilot phase completed – June 2027  
   

**Project manager and team, roles, and employment**   
**Project Manager (part-time, 12 months)**  
Responsible for organizing the project, managing the timeline and budget, coordinating partners, and preparing reports.

**Technical Lead (contract-based)**  
Responsible for developing and maintaining the online registry system.

**Inspection Coordinator (part-time)**  
Responsible for organizing inspector training and scheduling property inspections.

**Legal Advisor (external consultant)**  
Provides legal support related to certification rules, liability, and data protection.

**Steering Committee (internal members)**  
Provides supervision and approves key project decisions. Members participate as part of their regular university roles and are not additionally paid.

**Risk assessment**   
The project is assessed as medium risk due to possible low participation from landlords, dependency on institutional cooperation, legal liability concerns related to certification, and budget limitations.  These risks will be managed through clear legal guidelines, institutional support, phased implementation, and awareness campaigns to encourage participation.

# **4\. Hypothesis Testing** {#4.-hypothesis-testing}
After defining the proposed functionality, we conducted interviews to validate whether the identified problems and assumptions reflected real user experiences. The goal of the hypothesis testing phase was to evaluate the relevance and practicality of the proposed solution from multiple perspectives.

## **Parent of a Student Tenant** {#parent-of-a-student-tenant}

**Person interviewed:**  
Age: 50  
Role: Parent of a university student  
Background: Her daughter is studying abroad, and they are looking for a new apartment.

**Questions and Answers**

**1\. How do you usually search for rental housing for your child?**  
 I usually search through online rental platforms and sometimes Facebook groups for students. I also look at Google Maps and reviews to see the location and the surrounding area.

# **8\. Engineering Management** {#8.-engineering-management}
In addition to technical and organizational planning, the project also required reflection on internal team dynamics and collaboration practices. This section analyzes both positive and negative management patterns observed during the project development process.

In our **Housing Quality & Safety Certification Program**, the most important patterns are team and stakeholder management patterns because the success of the project depends on communication, collaboration, trust, and continuous feedback between team members and stakeholders.

## **Negative Patterns** {#negative-patterns}

### **1\. No Clear Role Distribution**  {#1.-no-clear-role-distribution}

In our team, roles and responsibilities were never formally assigned. Everyone worked on tasks informally, without clear ownership, leading to confusion about who was responsible for what. 

**Why it’s problematic**

When no one owns a specific area, tasks either get duplicated or fall through the cracks entirely. Team members may also not understand their individual priorities, which makes it hard to plan or hold anyone accountable. 

**How we would handle it**

We assign explicit ownership for each area of the project. Each person knows what they are responsible for delivering, and this is written down on a shared task board rather than left to informal assumption.

### **2\. Uneven Workload Distribution**  {#2.-uneven-workload-distribution}

Without assigned roles or visible task tracking, work was distributed unevenly across the team, not intentionally, but as a natural consequence of the lack of structure. 

**Why it’s problematic**

Uneven distribution leads to some team members being overloaded while others are underutilized, which slows the project and can affect team morale over time. 

**How we would handle it**

We started discussing our work more openly as a team and making decisions together, making sure everyone is aligned before moving forward. We also introduced a practice of reviewing each other's work, so that no task depends entirely on one person. This way, if someone is stuck or unavailable, the rest of the team can step in without losing progress. 

### **3\. Lack of Communication**  {#3.-lack-of-communication}

Our team communicated exclusively online and reactively; messages were sent only after something was already done, rather than before or during the work. 

**Why it’s problematic**

The Housing Quality & Safety Certification Program involves multiple moving parts, such as stakeholder interviews, criteria design, documentation, and prototyping. Because we only communicated after the fact, the team couldn't coordinate in advance or catch misalignments early. People worked based on their own interpretation of priorities, and by the time updates were shared, it was often too late to adjust without rework. 

**How we would handle it**

We shift from reactive to proactive communication, which is discussing plans and intentions before starting work, not just reporting results afterward. This means agreeing on what each person will do at the start of the week, so the team can flag overlaps or conflicts early. Even if communication stays online, it needs to happen at the planning stage, not only at the delivery stage. 

## **Positive Patterns** {#positive-patterns}

### **1\. Lose the Ego and Resolve Conflicts Constructively**  {#1.-lose-the-ego-and-resolve-conflicts-constructively}

In a small team working on a shared project, personal ambitions and individual preferences need to take a back seat to what is best for the project as a whole. Instead of avoiding disagreements, the team treats differences of opinion as opportunities to make better decisions together. 

**Why it’s effective**

When team members are willing to let go of the need to be right, decisions get made faster and with less friction. Addressing disagreements openly and respectfully prevents tension from building up and ensures that decisions reflect the whole team's perspective, not just the loudest voice. It also creates an environment where everyone feels comfortable contributing, because no one is afraid of being dismissed or overshadowed. 

**How we implement it**

When we disagree on an approach, we evaluate options based on what best serves the project rather than on who proposed it. If someone's idea is not chosen, that is treated as a normal part of the process, not a personal loss. 

### **2\. Continuous Feedback and Improvement** {#2.-continuous-feedback-and-improvement}

The team regularly reflects on its work and adjusts processes based on what is and is not working. 

**Why it’s effective**

A certification program with real stakeholders requires the team to adapt as requirements become clearer and feedback comes in. Without deliberate reflection, the same inefficiencies repeat across sprints, and the final solution risks missing actual user needs. 

**How we implement it**

At the end of each sprint, we conduct a short retrospective on what went well, what did not, and what we will do differently next time. We also review stakeholder feedback regularly and use it to adjust priorities. 

### **3\. Be a Catalyst**  {#3.-be-a-catalyst}

A good team member does not just complete their own tasks; they actively help the rest of the team move forward by removing blockers and keeping momentum going. 

**Why it’s effective**

In a project with multiple moving parts and external dependencies, progress can stall when someone is waiting on a response from a stakeholder, stuck on a technical issue, or unsure how to proceed. If no one steps in, that delay spreads to the whole team. 

**How we implement it**

When a team member is blocked, others step in to help resolve the issue rather than waiting for it to sort itself out. This might mean chasing a stakeholder response, helping clarify a requirement, or taking over a task temporarily so the project keeps moving. The goal is for the whole team to feel responsible for overall progress, not just their individual piece.
